\section{Products interacting with sums}
\label{sec:arithemetic-products-sums}


The statement
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sets-distributor}
    (\setA \cartprod \setB) \setdisunion (\setA \cartprod \setC)  \isomorphic \setA \cartprod (\setB \setdisunion \setC).
\end{equation}
is analogous to the \emph{distributivity} property of multiplication and addition of numbers:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:arithmetic-distributive-law}
    (x \cdot y) + (x \cdot z)  = x \cdot (y + z).
\end{equation}
